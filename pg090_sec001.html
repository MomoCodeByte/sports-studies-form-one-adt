Starting 1500 m race

The starting position in the 1500 m track event is the same as in the 800 m, where an athlete must start from standing stance and observe similar procedures as in 800 m.

Running 1500 m race

A proper form of running 1500 m is essential. Running correctly will help an athlete to save energy and run more efficiently, quickly and confidently. The following are the techniques used to complete the 1500 m race:

Look forward

When running 1500 m, look straight ahead for at least 30 to 40 m to help to maintain speed and reduce shoulder and neck discomfort. This relaxes the neck and shoulders and allows blood to travel easily to the brain; this enables the runner to continue running for a long distance.

Keep shoulders relaxed

Shoulders should be down and back, if you see your shoulders hunching, immediately make them relax.

Relax your hands

Avoid tightening the hands into fists when running; instead, keep them relaxed with the thumb resting on the top of the fingers. This allows muscles to relax, enabling oxygenated blood to circulate throughout the body to continue running for a long distance.

Bend your elbows at 90°

While running, keep the arms relaxed at the sides with the elbows bent at 90°. Swing the hands forward and back in a straight manner and allow arms to cross in front of the chest. The contrasting legs and arms stimulate muscle fibers to generate energy and continue striding.

Lean slightly forward

Keep the back straight and lean slightly toward the middle of the foot as you are running; this will help to propel forward and land on the middle of the foot rather than the heel.

Level your knees

When running, avoid lifting the knees higher than the hips. When the foot hits the ground, aim to land with a little bend in the knee to absorb some of the impact. This helps to reduce the potential problems such as muscular cramps when landing with strong impact.
