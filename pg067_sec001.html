  2. (iii)

    Event-specific training: Athletes should receive proper training for their specific events to ensure proper form and reduce the risk of injuries;

  3. (iv)

    Adequate rest and recovery: Athletes should get enough rest and recovery between practices and events to prevent overuse injuries and fatigue;

  4. (v)

    Hydration: Athletes should drink enough water before, during, and after their practices and events, especially in hot weather;

  5. (vi)

    Event-specific equipment: Ensure that any equipment used in the events, such as starting blocks, hurdles, and batons are properly maintained in good condition;

  6. (vii)

    Event specific rules and regulations: Athletes should be familiar with the rules and regulations of their events to avoid unnecessary accidents and disqualifications;

  7. (viii)

    Know the track: Athletes should be familiar with the track layout, curves, and markings to anticipate their movements and reduce the risk of collisions;

  8. (ix)

    Safe environment: The track and field area should be clear of debris, obstacles, or any hazardous material that can cause accidents; and

  9. (x)

    Appropriate clothing: Athletes should wear comfortable and appropriate clothes that allow for a full range of motion and do not pose a safety risk.

Short distance running

This is a group of running events that take place over a short distance. The short distance races are sprints, relays and hurdles.

Sprints

Sprints involve running at full speed throughout the race. The sprint events include 100 m, 200 m and 400 m distances. While 100 m sprints begin in a straight lane, the 200 m and 400 m start in a curved lane, as shown in Figure 5.1.
