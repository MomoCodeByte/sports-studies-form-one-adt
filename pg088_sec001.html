Activity 5.17

Use various drills to practise standing start technique in 800 m race repetitively to master the skill.

Question:

What is the essence of using a standing start in 800 m race?

Running 800 m race

Runners are allowed to break in to the inner lane after running the first 100 m in a curved lane. The 800 m can be divided into four sections, namely first 200 m, 200 to 400 m, 400 to 600 m, and 600 to 800 m.

(a) In the first 200 m, you should get out with a good pace and avoid going to the inner lane immediately when comes off the first curve. The runner should also aim to start the next curve while running in straight line;

(b) From 200 to 400 m, stays relaxed and fast. The runner pushes forward gradually so as to be fast;

(c) From 400 to 600 m, consciously picks up the pace, runs fast and smoothly; and

(d) From 600 to 800 m, stays as smooth and keeps the running form together and pushes forward. This section ends up the 800 m race.

Techniques to be developed for better performance include action of the head, shoulder, body, arms, leading leg and back leg.

Action of the head

The head should be kept still to avoid tightening the neck and shoulder muscles. The eyes should look at the ground at least 10 m in front of the athlete; the mouth should be slightly open to improve inhaling and increase the generation of energy in the muscles to keep on running over the required distance.

Action of arms

The arms should be held at an angle of approximately greater than 90° and moved beneath the shoulders from the front to the rear and keep on swinging to drive forward and help to cover a longer distance with maximum energy and speed.
