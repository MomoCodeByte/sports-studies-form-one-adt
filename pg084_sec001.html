(iii) By pressing the thigh down and backward, the down movement can be quickened; and

(iv) Active (quick) landing ensures the best get-away stride.

Action of the trailing leg

(i) After vigorous push-off from the ground, the leg relaxed for the take-off; and

(ii) The trailing leg must be stretched away from the body when clearing the hurdle.

Action of the arms

(i) The arms maintain the balance during running;

(ii) The arm on the opposite side of the leading leg must aid the realisation of a proper forward lean of the trunk;

(iii) When the trail leg comes forward, the trailing arm swings backward to counterbalance the movement of the get-away stride;

(iv) After landing, the arms give an immediate effective support to the get-away stride; and

(v) A favourable forward lean implies that the hand of the leading arm stretches to the level of the foot of the leading leg. The eyes are facing ahead during the hurdle stride.

Length of the hurdle stride

(i) The hurdle stride is about 3.5 m length of which 2.1 m applies to the track distance before and 1.4 m to the distance behind the hurdle;

(ii) Flight time is shortened only by quick landing of the leading leg; and

(iii) Shortening the distance of landing behind the next hurdle and a relative increase between the hurdles.

Three-step rhythm between the hurdles

(i) The runner has to move three stride between the hurdles;

(ii) The first stride after landing, which is about 1.55–1.60 m, is always the shortest distance because the push-off force is reduced by the preceding hurdle stride;

(iii) The second stride, which is about 2.0 to 2.2 m, is the longest stride between the hurdles; and

(iv) The last stride before the hurdle is the shortest than the previous one.
