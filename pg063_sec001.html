Law no.9: Ball in and out of play

When the ball is inside the field of play and the referee has not yet halted play, the ball is in play. When the ball, whether in the air or on the ground, completely crosses the sidelines or the goal lines, it is no longer in play. The ball is still in play if it bounces off a goalpost, crossbar, corner flag post, the official or one of the assistant officials and stays within the playing area.

Law no.10: Methods of scoring

If there are no further violations, a goal is scored when the ball completely crosses the goal line between the goalposts and under the crossbar. The team that scores the most wins the game. The game is deemed a draw if neither team scores more goals than the other, or if no goals are scored at all.

Law no.11: Offside

When a ball is passed forward, a player is considered offside, if he or she is in the opposing team’s half of the field; closer to the opposing team’s goal line than the ball; and there are no more than two defenders (including the goalkeeper) closer to the goal line than the attacking player. Therefore, the attacking player between the ball and the last defender near the opposition’s goal line will be in the offside position. When a player is flagged for being offside, the opposing team is given a free kick.

Law no.12: Fouls and misconducts

A player has committed a foul if recklessly trips, kicks, pushes, or charges another player; strikes, tries to strike, or spits at them; makes a tackle but makes contact with the opponent before the ball; purposefully hands the ball (goalkeepers in their area exempted); or obstructs an opponent or prevents them from releasing the ball.

Law no.13: Free-kicks

After a foul or other violations, the play is restarted with a free-kick, which is typically taken from the spot where the offence was committed. Free kicks can be “direct,” where the taker scores immediately, or “indirect,” where a ball must first be touched by the taker and a second member of the same side.
