Law no.4: Player’s equipment

In football match, a T-shirt, shorts, stockings, shin guards, and football boots are the required items for players involved. Only goalkeepers are required to wear a uniform that differentiates them from other members of their team, rival teams, and referees. Head gear may be used provided it does not endanger other players. The majority of jewellery is prohibited.

Law no.5: The referee

The referee is the final arbiter and interpreter of the football rules in the game of football. The referee makes the call on whether a game to continue or not, and he has the authority to stop play if a player needs medical attention. He or she issues warnings (verbal or yellow cards), and expulsions (red cards), and is in charge of keeping track of time, keeping records, and making sure that all match gear and clothes are in order.

Law no.6: The assistant referees

The assistant referees, formerly called linesmen. They primarily support the referee by signaling for corner kicks, throw-ins, and offside infringements. They must also bring the referee’s attention to any other fouls or infringements that the referee may not have seen when committed. However, the referee’s decision is always final.

Law no.7: Duration of the match

A football match consists of 90 minutes with two halves of 45 minutes of play, and additional time may be added at the referee’s discretion in case of injuries, substitutions, and time wasting, as well as time can be added to allow a penalty to be taken after normal time. However, rules governing extra time can be established by national football federations.

Law no.8: Start and restart the game

The game begins with a tossing coin. The winners of a toss select the first half’s ends, while the loser’s kick-off. The restart of the game occurs during the start of the second half where the opposing team begins to play. The game can also restart each time the team score a goal. The centre point is used for the kick-off, and the ball must be moved into the half of the opponent. The opposition must be at least 9.15 m from the ball, and all players must be inside their half of the field. Before the first player may touch the ball again, a second player must touch it.
